04 / MFS · FINANCIAL STANDARDS · PRODUCT CONCEPT
MEZX PRODUCT PILLAR

Financial information, checked against the right standards.

MezX Financial Standards (MFS) is a proposed configurable verification and methodology engine. It is designed to evaluate financial calculations, accounting treatments and reporting outputs against selected standards, frameworks and jurisdiction-specific rules — with traceable references and reviewable findings.

Product status: MFS is a product direction and design concept, not a currently available compliance or certification service. Any future checks would depend on licensed or authoritative source material, jurisdiction, effective date, entity type, data quality and professional review.
01

Standards-based verification

Compare selected financial information and reporting treatments with the applicable requirements and methodology selected by the user.

02

Regional rule configuration

Apply country, tax authority, VAT/GST, customs, industry and reporting-period settings rather than assuming one global rule set.

03

Explainable findings

Show the affected item, rule reference, source version, calculation or rationale, and whether human review is required.

Framework library

Standards and frameworks within the MFS scope

These frameworks cover different domains. They should be configured as distinct rule families, not treated as interchangeable accounting standards.

01 / IFRS

International Financial Reporting Standards

Financial reporting requirements for recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure. A rules library would need to track the applicable standard, amendments, effective dates, transition provisions and jurisdictional adoption.

Domain: corporate financial reporting
02 / GAAP

Generally Accepted Accounting Principles

Accounting principles and authoritative requirements that vary by jurisdiction. MFS should identify the relevant GAAP (for example, US GAAP or another national framework) instead of treating “GAAP” as one universal standard.

Domain: jurisdiction-specific accounting
03 / ISSB

IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 — Sustainability Disclosure Standards

IFRS S1 addresses general sustainability-related financial disclosures; IFRS S2 addresses climate-related disclosures. Both were developed by the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB). Checks may cover governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets, subject to applicable adoption.

Domain: sustainability-related financial disclosures
04 / ISA

International Standards on Auditing

Auditing standards relevant to the conduct of independent audits. MFS could organize audit criteria, evidence references and selected procedure checks, but it cannot perform or certify an independent audit by itself.

Domain: external audit methodology
05 / BASEL

Basel III and the evolving Basel framework

International banking prudential standards covering areas such as capital adequacy, leverage, liquidity and risk measurement. “Basel IV” is commonly used as an industry label for the final Basel III reforms; it is not a separate official Basel framework title. Local implementation and effective dates matter.

Domain: banking prudential regulation
06 / FATF

FATF 40 Recommendations

International standards for combating money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing. MFS could support structured control mapping, evidence organization and gap indicators; it should not claim to determine legal compliance or replace a regulated institution’s risk-based controls.

Domain: AML/CFT/CPF framework mapping
07 / IOSCO

IOSCO Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation

Principles for securities-market regulation, including investor protection, fair and efficient markets, transparency and systemic-risk considerations. Mapping should be tailored to the regulator and market jurisdiction.

Domain: securities and capital-markets regulation
08 / OECD

G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance

Principles and recommendations concerning corporate governance, shareholder rights, disclosure, transparency, board responsibilities and sustainability/resilience. They are governance principles, not accounting calculation rules.

Domain: corporate governance
09 / WB

World Bank and UNCITRAL materials

Relevant legal, institutional, procurement, insolvency, secured-transactions and commercial-law frameworks or guidance may be mapped where a defined use case exists. These are broad bodies of work, not a single financial reporting standard.

Domain: legal and institutional reference frameworks
Jurisdiction modules

Regional financial and regulatory rules

Beyond international frameworks, MFS is intended to support configurable local rule packs. Each rule must be tied to a jurisdiction, issuing authority, effective date and source.

Taxation

Income tax treatments, tax computations, withholding requirements and reporting obligations, configured for the relevant country and taxpayer category.

VAT / GST

VAT/GST rates, taxable supplies, exemptions, input-credit conditions, invoice requirements, returns and reconciliation checks.

Customs and trade

Customs valuation, tariff classification references, duties, import/export documentation and relevant trade-policy rules where authoritative data is available.

Local accounting adoption

National adoption, local modifications, filing formats, regulator circulars and effective-date differences from international standards.

Sector-specific rules

Additional configuration for banking, insurance, listed entities, public-sector bodies or other regulated industries.

Version and source control

Maintain source citations, rule versions, effective periods, superseded rules and a record of which rule set was applied to each check.

How the engine is intended to work

From financial data to reviewable findings

A configurable workflow, with explicit assumptions and human review at the points where judgment is required.

Configure

Select jurisdiction, reporting period, entity type, industry, standards and applicable rule versions.

Ingest

Provide financial statements, ledgers, transactions, schedules or structured reporting data.

Validate

Run arithmetic, consistency, mapping and selected standards-based checks against the configured rule set.

Review

Inspect exceptions, source references, evidence and assumptions; route judgment-based items to qualified reviewers.

Output model

What a verification result should show

MFS should avoid a vague “pass/fail” score. Each finding needs enough context for a finance professional to understand and investigate it.

Mathematical validationArithmetic, totals, cross-footing and internal consistency.
Standards alignmentPotential mismatch with a selected accounting or reporting requirement.
Regulatory rule checkPotential issue under the selected jurisdiction and effective period.
Evidence and traceabilitySource document, rule reference, version, calculation and review notes.
Exception severityPriority and potential impact, without overstating legal conclusions.
Human review statusConfirmed, unresolved, not applicable or requiring expert judgment.

Important boundary: A software check can identify inconsistencies and potential deviations, but cannot guarantee that every requirement has been met.
